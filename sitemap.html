Week 9 Written Notes
Topics covered
  • Quantization of the Electromagnetic Field
    • Parameterized the vector potential: \begin{align} \vec A(\vec r, t) &= \sum_{\vec k, i} C^{-*}_{\vec k, i}(t) \hat \epsilon^*_{\vec k, i} e^{-i\vec k \cdot\vec r} + C^-_{\vec k, i}(t) \hat \epsilon_{\vec k, i} e^{i\vec k \cdot\vec r} \end{align} by finding that the vector potential must be in the plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation. Leads to the definition: \[\hat\epsilon_{k,1}, \quad \hat\epsilon_{k,2}, \quad \hat\epsilon_{k,i}\cdot\hat\epsilon_{k,j}^* = \delta_{ij}, \quad \hat\epsilon_{-\vec k, i} \equiv \hat\epsilon_{\vec k, i}^*, \quad \hat\epsilon_{k,i}\cdot\vec k = 0\] and expansion of the vector potential in this plane, with expansion coefficients \(A_{\vec k, i}(t) = A^*_{-\vec k, i}(t)\): \begin{align} A_{\vec k, i}(t) &= C^+_{\vec k, i}(t=0) e^{i\omega t} + C^-_{\vec k, i}(t=0) e^{-i\omega t} \\ &= C^+_{\vec k, i}(t) + C^-_{\vec k, i}(t) \tag{3} \end{align} where \( C^\pm_{\vec k, i}(t) = C^{\mp*}_{-\vec k, i}(t) \) and \(\omega = ck\).
    • Found canonically conjugated variables: \[q_{\vec k, i}(t) = \frac{1}{\omega\sqrt 2}(\alpha_{\vec k, i}(t) + \alpha^*_{\vec k, i}(t)) \text{ and } p_{\vec k, i}(t) = \frac{1}{i\sqrt 2}(\alpha_{\vec k, i}(t) - \alpha^*_{\vec k, i}(t))\] where \(H = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\vec k, i}(\omega^2 q^2_{\vec k, i}(t) + p^2_{\vec k, i}(t))\) and \(\alpha_{\vec k, i}(t) \equiv \sqrt{\frac{V\omega^2}{2\pi c^2}}C^-_{\vec k, i}(t) \in \mathbb C\) and \(H = \sum_{\vec k, i}\alpha_{\vec k, i}(t)\alpha^*_{\vec k, i}(t)\).
    • Quantized the EM field: \begin{align} q_{\vec k, i}(t) \mapsto \hat q_{\vec k, i}(t) &\text{ and } p_{\vec k, i}(t) \mapsto \hat p_{\vec k, i}(t) \\ q^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t) \mapsto \hat q^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t) &\text{ and } p^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t) \mapsto \hat p^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t) \\ [\hat q_{\vec k, i}(t), \hat q_{\vec k, j}(t)] &= [\hat p_{\vec k, i}(t), \hat p_{\vec k, j}(t)] = 0 \\ [\hat q_{\vec k, i}(t), \hat p_{\vec k', j}(t)] &= i\hbar\delta_{\vec k\vec k'}\delta_{ij} \\ H \mapsto \hat H &= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\vec k, i}(\omega^2 \hat q^2_{\vec k, i}(t) + \hat p^2_{\vec k, i}(t)) \end{align} Defined dimensionless operators: \[\hat Q_{\vec k, i}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{\omega}{\hbar}}\hat q_{\vec k, i}(t) \text{ and } \hat P_{\vec k, i}(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hbar\omega}}\hat p_{\vec k, i}(t)\] where \([\hat Q_{\vec k, i}(t), \hat P_{\vec k, i}(t)] = i\delta_{\vec k, \vec k'}\delta_{ij}\) and \(\hat H = \sum_{\vec k, i}\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}(\hat Q^2_{\vec k, i}(t) + \hat P^2_{\vec k, i}(t))\).

      Defined annihilation and creation operators: \[\hat a_{\vec k, i}(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(\hat Q_{\vec k, i}(t) + i\hat P_{\vec k, i}(t)) \text{ and } \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(\hat Q_{\vec k, i}(t) - i\hat P_{\vec k, i}(t))\] and number operator: \begin{align} \hat n_{\vec k, i}(t) &= \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t)\hat a_{\vec k, i}(t) \\ \hat n_{\vec k, i}(t)|n\rangle &= n\ket n, n\in \{0, 1, 2, \cdots\} \\ \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(t)\ket n = \sqrt{n+1}\ket {n+1}&, \hat a_{\vec k, i}(t)\ket n = \sqrt n\ket{n-1} \end{align} where \(\hat H = \sum_{\vec k, i} \hbar\omega(\hat n_{\vec k, i}(t) + \frac{1}{2})\).

      The EM field is thus infinite independent quantum harmonic oscillators. A state is wrtten as \(\ket\psi = \ket 1 \otimes \ket 0 \otimes \ket 1 \otimes \ket 7 \otimes \cdots = \ket{1,0,1,7,\cdots}\) or \(|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2!}}(\hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i})^2 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{5!}}(\hat a^\dagger_{\vec k', j})^5 \ket -\)
    • Defined the field operators: \begin{align} \hat {\vec A}(\vec r, t) &= \sum_{\vec k, i} \sqrt\frac{h c^2}{V\omega} \left[ \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i} \hat \epsilon^*_{\vec k, i} e^{-i\vec k \cdot\vec r} + \hat a_{\vec k, i} \hat \epsilon_{\vec k, i} e^{i\vec k \cdot\vec r}\right] \end{align} \begin{align} \hat {\vec E}_\perp &= \sum_{\vec k, i} i\sqrt{\frac{h\omega}{V}}\left(\hat a_{\vec k, i}\hat \epsilon_{\vec k, i} e^{i\vec k \cdot\vec r} - \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}\hat \epsilon^*_{\vec k, i} e^{-i\vec k \cdot\vec r}\right) \\ \vec B &= \sum_{\vec k, i} i\sqrt{\frac{h\omega}{V}} \left(\hat a_{\vec k, i}(\hat k \times\hat \epsilon_{\vec k, i}) e^{i\vec k \cdot\vec r}- \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}(\hat k \times\hat \epsilon^*_{\vec k, i}) e^{-i\vec k \cdot\vec r}\right) \end{align}

Week 10 Written Notes (had a medterm this Tuesday)
Topics covered
  • Quantization of the Electromagnetic Field: Momentum
    • Found operator for linear angular momentum using \(\hat T_{\Delta\vec r} = e^{-i\Delta\vec r\cdot\hat{\vec p}/\hbar}\) and \[ [\hat a_{\vec k, i}, \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}] = \delta_{\vec k, \vec k'}\delta_{ij} \qquad \text{and} \qquad [\hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}, \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k', j}] = [\hat a_{\vec k, i}, \hat a_{\vec k', j}] = 0 \] This lets us find \([\hat{\vec p}, \hat a_{\vec k, i}] = -\hbar\hat a_{\vec k, i}\vec k\) Which leads to \(\hat{\vec p} = \sum_{\vec k, i}\hbar\vec k \hat a^\dagger_{\vec k, i}\hat a_{\vec k, i} = \sum_{\vec k, i}\hat n_{\vec k, i} \cdot \hbar\vec k\).
    • Found operator for angular momentum using classical \(\vec J = \frac{1}{4\pi c}\int d^3 r \quad \vec r\times(\vec E_\perp \times \vec B)\) and found: \begin{align} \vec J &= \frac{1}{4\pi c}\int d^3 r \quad E_l(\vec r \times \vec\nabla) A_l - \frac{1}{4\pi c}\int d^3 r \quad \hat u_i\varepsilon_{ijk} r_j(\vec E_\perp\cdot\vec\nabla) A_k \\ \end{align} First term is orbital angular momentum, second term is spin: \[ \frac{1}{4\pi c}\int d^3 r \vec E_\perp \times \vec A \]
  • Circular Polarization Expressed the spin operator in terms of circularly polarized fields: \begin{align} \hat{\vec J}_s = \sum_{\vec k} \hbar\hat k (\hat n_{\vec k, +} - \hat n_{\vec k, -}) \end{align} for polarization vectors: \[\hat \epsilon_{\vec k, \pm} \equiv \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(\hat u_{\vec k, 1} \pm i\hat u_{\vec k, 2})\] which shows that photons are spin-1 particles.
Week 11 Written Notes
Topics covered
  • Quantization of the Electromagnetic Field: Coherent states Coherent states defined as: \begin{align} \hat a |\alpha\rangle = \alpha|\alpha\rangle, &\quad |\alpha\rangle = e^{-\alpha^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}} |n\rangle, \quad \alpha\in\mathbb C \\ \langle \alpha | \hat a | \alpha\rangle = \alpha, &\quad \langle \alpha | \hat a^\dagger | \alpha\rangle = \alpha^*, \quad \langle \alpha_{\vec k, i} | \hat a_{\vec k', j} | \alpha_{\vec k, i} \rangle = \delta_{\vec k, \vec k'}\delta_{ij}\alpha_{\vec k, i} \end{align} and evolve over time as: \[|\psi(t=0)\rangle = |\alpha\rangle, \quad |\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i\hat H t/\hbar}|\alpha\rangle = |\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\rangle = |\alpha(t)\rangle\] approximate classical states. We show that the vector potential, electric field, and magnetic field operators do not always have an expected value of 0.
  • The Total Hamiltonian for Radiation and Matter Introduced interaction Hamiltonian between field and matter systems: \[ \hat H = \hat H_{rad} + \hat H_{matter} + \hat H' \] where time-idenpendent interaction is: \[\hat H' = -\sum_i \frac{q_i}{m_i c} \cdot \hat{\vec A}(\hat{\vec r}_i)\cdot\hat{\vec p_i} + \sum_i\frac{q_i^2}{2m_i c^2} \hat{\vec A}^2(\hat{\vec r}_i)\] or under dipole approximation and velocity gauge, \[\hat H' \approx -\sum_i \frac{q_i}{m_i c} \cdot \hat{\vec A}(\vec 0)\cdot\hat{\vec p_i}\] Used TDPT generalization \(\mathcal A_{f\leftarrow i}^{(n)} = \frac{2\pi}{i\hbar}\langle f | \hat O \left[\hat G_0^+(E_i)\hat O\right]^{n-1} | i\rangle \delta_T(\omega_{fi})\) for \(\hat O = \hat H'\) to do an example.
  • Spontaneous Emission Used TDPT generalization where \(\hat O = -\hat{\vec {\mathcal E}} \cdot \hat{\vec\mu}\) (electric-dipole/length-gauge) to show that transition rate for spontaneous emission is non-zero. This requires identity: \[\sum_{\vec k} = \frac{V}{(2\pi)^3}\int d^3k\] Total transition rate is \(\frac{4\omega^3}{3\hbar c^3}|\vec\mu_{ge}|^2\).
  • Introduction to Scattering Theory
    • Introduced notion of eigenstates of \(\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat V\), \(|\psi^{(\pm)}_{\vec k, i}\rangle\) that are controlled in the past and future, and wavepackets of these states.
    • Briefly mentioned transition amplitudes between these eigenstates, which is determined by the scattering matrix: \[\mathcal A_{\vec k_f\leftarrow \vec k_0} \propto \delta(E' - E) \mathcal S_{\beta\alpha}(E)\] TODO: look at hw that has us find the scattering matrix
    • A quick derivation of the TILSE, which we do in more depth in week 12.
    • Briefly saw the T-matrix: \[T_{\beta\alpha}(E) = \langle\phi_{\beta E} | \hat V | \psi^+_{\alpha E}\rangle = \langle\phi_{\beta E} | \hat V \left[\hat G_0^+(E)\hat V\right]^n | \phi_{\alpha E}\rangle\]
    • and the Born series: \begin{align} |\psi_{\alpha E}^\pm\rangle &= |\phi_{\alpha E}\rangle + \hat G_0^\pm(E)\hat V|\psi^\pm_E\rangle \\ &= \sum_{n=0}^\infty \left[\hat G_0^\pm(E)\hat V\right]^n|\phi_{\alpha E}\rangle \end{align}
Week 12 Written Notes
Topics covered
  • Scattering Theory: Möller Operators
    • Möller operators match unperturbed generalized eigenstates to perturbed, full Hamiltonian generalized eigenstates: \begin{align} \hat\Omega_+ &= \lim_{t\to-\infty}e^{i\hat Ht/\hbar}e^{-i\hat H_0t/\hbar}, \quad |\psi^{(+)}\rangle = \hat\Omega_+|\phi_{in}(t_f)\rangle \\ \hat\Omega_- &= \lim_{t\to+\infty}e^{i\hat Ht/\hbar}e^{-i\hat H_0t/\hbar}, \quad |\psi^{(-)}\rangle = \hat\Omega_-|\phi_{out}(t_0)\rangle \end{align}
    • Intertwining relations: \(\hat H \hat\Omega_\pm = \hat\Omega_\pm\hat H_0\)

      This leads to \(\sigma_C(\hat H_0) = \sigma_C(\hat H)\).
  • Scattering Theory: Lipmann-Schwinger Equations
    • First version of TDLSE: \begin{align} |\psi^{(+)}(t)\rangle &= |\phi_{in}(t)\rangle + \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{-\infty}^t e^{-i\hat H_0 (t-t')/\hbar} \hat V |\psi^{(+)}(t')\rangle dt' \\ \end{align}
    • Second version of TDLSE: \begin{align} |\psi^{(+)}(t)\rangle &= |\phi_{in}(t)\rangle + \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{-\infty}^t e^{-i\hat H (t-t')/\hbar} \hat V |\phi_{in}(t')\rangle dt' \\ \end{align}
    • For generalized eigenstates: \begin{align} |\psi^{(+)}(t)\rangle &= |\phi_{in}(t)\rangle + \frac{1}{i\hbar} \lim_{\varepsilon\to0^+} \int_{-\infty}^t e^{-\varepsilon (t-t')/\hbar} e^{-i\hat H_0 (t-t')/\hbar} \hat V |\psi^{(+)}(t')\rangle dt' \\ |\psi^{(+)}(t)\rangle &= |\phi_{in}(t)\rangle + \frac{1}{i\hbar} \lim_{\varepsilon\to0^+} \int_{-\infty}^t e^{-\varepsilon (t-t')/\hbar} e^{-i\hat H (t-t')/\hbar} \hat V |\phi_{in}(t')\rangle dt' \\ \end{align}
    • TILSE: \begin{align} |\psi^{(+)}\rangle &= |\phi_{in}\rangle + \hat G_0^+(E) \hat V |\psi^{(+)}\rangle \\ \end{align}
  • Scattering of a Massive Particle off a Potential in 3D Used TILSE in the form \(|\psi^+_{\vec k}\rangle = |\vec k\rangle + \hat G^+_0(E)\hat V|\psi^+_{\vec k}\rangle\) to solve for \(|\psi^+_{\vec k}\rangle\) for arbitrary 3D potential. Result: \begin{align} \psi^+_{\vec k}(\vec r) &= \frac{e^{i\vec k\cdot\vec r}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}} - \frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2} \int d^3r' V(\vec r')\psi^+_{\vec k}(\vec r') \frac{e^{ik|\vec r - \vec r'|}}{|\vec r-\vec r'|} \end{align} one clean incoming plane wave, and many spherical waves, one originating from each \(\vec r'\) where the potential is non-zero. At large distances from interaction center, approximates one large spherical wave. Forward scattered component cancels out the incoming plane wave (mathematically).

    First Born approximation shows that potential has a distribution of momenta and that interaction gives a momentum boost.

    Scattering cross section for solid angle at \(kr\gg 1\) using \(\rho = |\psi(\vec r)|^2\), \(\dot\rho = -\vec\nabla\cdot\vec j\), and \(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \vec j_{out}/\vec j_{in}\): \[ \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{(2\pi)^4 m^2}{\hbar^4}|T_{\vec k', \vec k}|^2 \]
Week 13 Written Notes
Topics covered
  • Miscellaneous Points on scattering
    • Used the TILSE on a time-dependent wavepacket constructed out of \(|\psi^+_{\alpha E}\rangle\).

      For sufficiently large and negative time, the wavepacket is \(|\psi(t)\rangle = \sum_\alpha\int dE e^{-iEt/\hbar}|\phi_{\alpha E}\rangle c_{\alpha E}\). This is a linear combination of unperturbed eigenstates/plane waves, with the same expansion coefficients used to construct the wavepacket out of \(|\psi^+_{\alpha E}\rangle\).

      In other words, \(\langle \psi^+_{\alpha E}|\psi(t)\rangle \approx \langle\phi_{\alpha E} | \psi(t)\rangle\) for sufficiently large and negative time.

    • Used the TILSE and the fact that \(|\psi_{\alpha E}^\pm\rangle\) form a complete basis in the full Hamiltonian's Hilbert space.

      Found that \( |\psi_{\alpha E}^+\rangle = \sum_\beta |\psi^-_{\beta E}\rangle (\delta_{\alpha\beta}-2i\pi T_{\beta\alpha}(E)) \) for on-shell T-matrix element \(T_{\beta\alpha}(E)\), and on-shell scattering matrix element \(\mathcal S_{\beta\alpha}(E) = \delta_{\alpha\beta}-2i\pi T_{\beta\alpha}(E)\).

    • Introduced the reduced radial function \(u_{EL}(r)\) for spherical potential: \[ \psi_{Elm}(\vec r) = R_{El}(r)Y_{lm}(\hat R), \quad R_{El} = \frac{u_{EL}(r)}{r} \] and radial TDSE: \[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}u''_{El} + \frac{\hbar^2}{2mr^2} l(l+1)u_{El} + V(r)u_{El} = E u_{El} \] which tells us that \(u_{El} \approx \sin(kr-l\pi/2 + \delta_l(E))\). Scattering phase shift \(\delta_l(E)\) describes how the potential affects the asymptotic behavior after scattering.

      Introduced the spherical Bessel functions which are eigenvectors of the unperturbed Hamiltonian and spherical waves: \[ j_l(kr) = \frac{\sin(kr-l\pi/2)}{kr} \] A linear combination of \(j_l\) can make up a plane wave: \[ e^{i\vec k\cdot\vec r} = 4\pi\sum_{l=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-l}^l i^l j_l(kr) Y^*_{lm}(\hat k) Y_{lm}(\hat r) \] If we substitute in \(e^{i\delta_l}\frac{\sin(kr-l\pi/2 + \delta_l(E))}{kr}\) for \(j_l\), we get \((2\pi)^{3/2}\psi^+_{\vec k}\).

      It is clear that we only need to know \(\delta_l(E)\) to know the scattering cross section. If \(\delta_l(E)\) is 0 for all \(l\) the potential has negligible asymptotic effect. At low energies, only \(l=0\) particles interact with the potential, and the scattering cross-section converges on a constant.
